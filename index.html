How I’d think about
AI, skills, and pay.

Should we pay more for AI skills? I’d start one step earlier. Is AI changing the value of a skill, the scope of an existing job, or creating a genuinely new one? Each answer leads to a different decision.

How I’d approach it

Start with the work,
not the title.

  1. 01
    Understand the work

    Sit with the business and map where AI is actually changing day-to-day responsibilities, and which skills now matter more or less. Two people using AI tools is not a pay decision. A real change in market value or scope might be.

  2. 02
    Name what changed

    Most cases land in one of four buckets, and each points to a different tool:

    • a skill that belongs inside an existing job
    • a scarce capability that may justify differentiated pay
    • a real change in scope that could affect level
    • genuinely new work that needs its own benchmark and architecture
  3. 03
    Test the market

    For emerging roles, surveys lag. So triangulate: what the role actually does, the talent pools it hires from, comparable jobs already in the architecture, external postings, internal peers, and how scarce the skills really are.

  4. 04
    Recommend the smallest change that fits

    Sometimes that’s nothing. Sometimes it’s an updated job profile, differentiated pay for a scarce skill, a level review, or a new job. Pay for durable market value and real differences in work, not for familiarity with a new tool.

  5. 05
    Bring leaders a decision, not a lecture

    What’s changing, where the risk is, what the market says, the options, a recommendation, the cost, and what happens if we do nothing.

A concrete example

Forward Deployed
Engineer.

A newer role spreading through AI-focused GTM teams: engineers who build in the customer’s environment and own the deployment. Public postings for the same title describe very different jobs, at base pay from roughly $105k to $385k.

So before pricing it, I’d pin down what it actually is.

Engineering? Solutions? Consulting? Implementation? A hybrid?

That answer sets everything downstream:

job family → level → benchmark → career path → pay

I wouldn’t pretend to know the answer from outside. The point is the method for finding it.

Where I’ve done
this before.

  • Job architecture

    Led an AI-assisted refresh of the go-to-market job architecture: hundreds of role cards restructured into new job families, groups, and titles, signed off by compensation leads in every business unit ahead of a Workday implementation.

  • Benchmarking

    Traced rising attrition across APJC roles to market reference ranges that had drifted below peer benchmarks, and made the case to recalibrate.

  • Executive consultation

    Advised senior leaders across a ~25,000-person GTM organization on compensation structure, workforce planning, and resource allocation, including compensation budget rollups at the VP and SVP level.

  • Decision tools

    Built what the systems didn’t offer: a modeling tool for out-of-range offers, and the Tableau dashboard leaders used to map acquired Splunk roles into Cisco’s architecture.

  • The recurring work

    Market reference ranges, incentive target administration, survey participation, and a steady volume of offer and retention questions.
