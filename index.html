Market pricingCisco go-to-market

Attrition was the signal.

One region kept losing people, across go-to-market, operations, and engineering at once, and the open roles behind them were not filling. When I re-plotted the market reference ranges for those roles against the peer median, they had drifted below it. Pay and offers both live inside the range, so the people had drifted below market and the offers were losing to it.

A five-step investigation: attrition concentrates in one region across several functions and hiring there stalls, range midpoints are re-plotted against the peer median, the ranges have drifted below it, and the recommendation is to recalibrate them and review the people inside them.

1 of 5

Illustrative data. The reasoning is real. Structure adjustments move ranges on an annual cycle, a fast-moving labor market outruns them, and the gap compounds.

Offer governancea working model

So each one gets the same three questions.

Out-of-range offers were being argued case by case, so I built a model that gave the same question the same treatment every time. This is a working version of it: the structure, the market, and the individual in one view, priced on on-target earnings, with a fictional candidate and public 2026 market data. Pick a role, slide the offer, and argue with it.

Geographic pay zone
In this model, multipliers apply to OTE: 1.00 for the Bay Area, New York, and Seattle, 0.92 for Boston, LA, San Diego, DC, Austin, Chicago, and Denver, 0.85 for the rest of the US. Differentials follow the cost of labor, not the cost of living.
Market targetmatch the market
The range midpoint is anchored to this market percentile. The target is a philosophy choice that varies by job family, business criticality, talent scarcity, geography, internal equity, and affordability. The model exposes the choice rather than asserting one.
Candidate against the role
Placement guideline by thirds of the range. If a candidate does not meet the role, the fix is the level, not the placement.

What is the organization telling us?

Before compensation, I worked on the organization itself: a global workforce strategy program across Cisco’s Sales and Finance functions. Attrition says who is leaving. Time-in-grade says who is waiting. Grade distribution says whether the shape of the organization matches its work. Read together, they became org design recommendations for both functions, and the business case for Cisco’s FY2022 org design playbook, the guide leaders would use the next time they restructured.
