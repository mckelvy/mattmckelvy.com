02

Four problems, taken apart.

Real problems from the last five years, rebuilt here as working exhibits. The data is synthetic — the real analyses stay at Cisco. The shape of the thinking is the point.

Case 01

The attrition nobody could explain.

Across the APJC region, people kept leaving — sales, operations, engineering, all at once. Nothing local explained it: not managers, not workload, not the narrative of the quarter.

Outcome. Made the case to leadership; the ranges were corrected. The lesson that stuck: attrition is often a pricing signal wearing an HR costume.

Case 02

Job architecture, rebuilt with AI.

Ahead of Cisco’s Workday implementation, the GTM job architecture needed a full rebuild: hundreds of role cards, inconsistent titles, families that had drifted apart through a decade of reorganization.

Outcome. AI did the reading — scraping and structuring hundreds of role cards into new families, groups, and titles. I did the taxonomy, then carried it through sign-off with compensation leads in every business unit. Volume from the machine; judgment from a human.

Case 03

The offer that doesn’t fit the range.

Out-of-range offers were being argued from scattered evidence — a screenshot here, a gut feeling there — while recruiters waited on deadline. So I built the instrument: market data, range positioning, and internal peers on one screen. This is a synthetic re-creation you can drive.

Case 04

Two companies, one architecture.

Cisco acquired Splunk — thousands of people arriving with their own levels, titles, and pay ranges. Every leveling call needed a translation both sides could trust.

Outcome. A Tableau dashboard mapped acquired roles against Cisco’s architecture and ranges — one interface for every post-acquisition leveling and pay decision. M&A compensation is translation work; you need a dictionary both sides trust.
