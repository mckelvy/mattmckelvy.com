01 / 04 · SIGNAL Market pricing · one APJC GTM function

one country separated from the others,

Attrition was one signal. Hiring was the other.

Both pointed to the range.

One GTM function in APJC was seeing elevated attrition in one country, and the roles left open behind it were not filling. I compared the same work across Country A, Country B, and Country C. The pattern pointed to the market reference ranges: Country A had drifted below the relevant peer median. Incumbent pay sat lower inside those ranges, while new offers were competing from the same disadvantaged position.

  1. SignalOne GTM function shows elevated attrition and persistent vacancies.
  2. CompareSame work, Country A vs. Country B vs. Country C.
  3. Re-plotRange midpoints against the relevant peer-market median.
  4. FindingCountry A’s ranges had drifted below market.
  5. RecommendationRecalibrate the ranges, then review incumbent positioning and hiring offers.

03 / 04 · DECISIONS Offer governance · a working model

exceptions should not depend on who tells the best story.

Make the decision system visible.

Out-of-range offers were being decided case by case. I built a model that put the same evidence in front of each decision: external market data, the candidate’s position in the range, and internal peers. The version here uses a fictional candidate and public 2026 market data. The decision logic is the same.

Headcount Tenure Attrition Time-in-grade Level mix One picture

Read the organization as a system.

Before compensation, I worked on workforce strategy across Cisco’s Sales and Finance functions.
